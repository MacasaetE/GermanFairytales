Grimm's to Gryffindor

Many of the fairy tales we grew up with originate from the Grimm Brother's collection of fairy tales.

Fairy tales such as Frau Holle illustrate that a girl's value is in her appearance and her work. Rather than a person with desires and goals, her body is a tool for others to use. Her less attractive sister refuses to be obedient, and is thus punished with a bucket of pitch.
In Sleeping Beauty, the princess is gifted with beauty and song before being cursed. The princess becomes more of an object to admire and guard than a woman coming of age. She is put to sleep only to be awoken by a non-consensual kiss she is supposed to be grateful for.
Cinderella is a character who is beautiful and kind. Despite her apparent beauty, no one is able to recognize her when she isn't covered in soot. This suggests that the story would not have ended so well for her if she hadn't been beautiful enough to catch the prince's eye, for he never would have looked at her as she had been.

    A Timeline

    Beauty Standards in Fairy Tales over time.

  • 1756

    Beauty and the Beast

    The prideful prince is punished with the appearance of an ugly beast, and can only be saved by a beautiful virgin loving him as he is. This reinforces the idea that outward appearance is a reflection of inner morality or 'goodness'. Despite receiving the consequences of his own actions, it falls on a woman to save him rather, than his own personal growth and reflection.

  • 1812

    Children's and Household Tales

    The Grimms' release the first edition of their collection of fairy tales. This collection reflects the ideology of gender rolls, praising girls for their beauty, obedience, and purity, while boys are praised for their courage, strength, and intelligence. Many of these tales have been adapted into children's books and films today.

  • 1837

    The Little Mermaid

    Andersen releases The Little Mermaid. He repeatedly refers to the little mermaid's skin as 'white'. This indicates a purity, beauty, and overall morality that is associated with whiteness. This is in contrast to evil, often symbolized by the color black.
    • "Now she cut her breast and let the black blood drip down into the kettle. The steam made the strangest shapes that were terrifying and dreadful to see." (266)
    • "...a lovely little mermaid was standing down below stretching her white hands up toward the keel." (254)
    • "...and then she saw that her fish tail was gone, and she had the prettiest little white legs that any young girl could have" (267)
    • "Then the little mermaid raised her beautiful white arms, stood up on her toes, and glided across the floor." (267)
    Andersen focuses on the beauty of the little mermaid. The mermaid's voice is taken from her and thus has to rely on her body to win the prince's love and gain an immortal soul.
  • 1937

    Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs

    Disney's first feature length film. Snow White's value is in her snow white skin, blood red lips, and jet black hair. In fact, her beauty is so great that upon turning seven-years-old, she surpassed her step-mother's beauty. This enraged her step-mother and incited her jealousy. This demonstrates that even a little girl's value is in her appearance. A grown woman should not be jealous of a little girl.

  • 1972

    Research Indicates

    When students were shown a series of pictures depicting different people, the attractive faces were rated as kinder, smarter, and more capable. This research aptly demonstrates the strength of the relationship between appearance and assumed morality. People naturally link beauty with goodness and unattractiveness with evil. (Dion et al., 1972)

  • 2009

    The Princess and The Frog

    The first black Disney princess was a huge milestone. However, fans were left disappointed when the main characters spent most of the movie as frogs. This demonstrates to audiences that non-white bodies are less important and less relatable than white bodies.
