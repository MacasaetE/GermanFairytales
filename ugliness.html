Ugliness as a Punishment

Fairy tales often use changes in appearance as a form of punishment.

Beauty and The Beast by Leprince de Beaumont:

"if my presence is troublesome, and I will immediately withdraw: but, tell me, do not you think me very ugly? That is true, said Beauty, for I cannot tell a lie, but I believe you are very good-natured. So I am, said the monster, but then, besides my ugliness, I have no sense; I know very well that I am a poor, silly, stupid creature." (143)

Frog Prince by the Brothers Grimm:

"When she woke up the next morning, she thought the frog would hop off again. Instead, she saw a handsome young prince standing before her, and he told her that he had been the bewitched frog and that she had saved him because she had promised to be his sweetheart. Then the two of them went to the king, who gave them his blessing, and a wedding was held. Meanwhile, the two other sisters were angry with themselves because they had not taken the frog to be their sweetheart." (333)

The prince is turned into a frog for some unknown reason. It is difficult to not judge a frog by its appearance, but that is what the main character is expected to do. She must court the frog to make him handsome again.
